NSF REU · UC Riverside · Summer 2024

Interpretable Modeling for High-Dimensional Data:
Comparing Lasso Regression and Neural Networks

Overview

This research, conducted at UC Riverside's NSF REU site "Experience the Full Data Science Pipeline through Research and Practice" (Grant No. 2244480), examined when a simple, interpretable model can match or outperform complex deep learning approaches on high-dimensional data — data where features greatly outnumber observations.

High-dimensional settings introduce well-known challenges: overfitting, computational burden, and difficulty understanding why a model makes a given prediction. While CNNs and FCNNs excel at capturing nonlinear patterns, their opacity limits use in fields where the decision-making process must be transparent, such as medicine and finance. Lasso regression offers a built-in feature selection mechanism and fully interpretable coefficients. The central question was: how much predictive power do you actually give up for that transparency?

Datasets

  • Simulation data — synthetic dataset designed to evaluate feature selection in a controlled setting where ground truth is known
  • Wave energy farm — real-world regression task predicting energy output from buoy configurations
  • TCGA stomach cancer — high-dimensional genomic data for binary classification of disease-specific survival
  • Taiwanese bankruptcy — financial dataset for predicting company bankruptcy

Key Findings

Lasso regression demonstrated competitive accuracy across all four datasets while requiring far less computation for both training and post-hoc explainability. Neural networks (CNNs and FCNNs) achieved marginal predictive gains in some settings but demanded substantially more resources when explaining predictions via SHAP values. The results support a practical principle: in high-stakes domains, reaching for the interpretable model first is defensible — and often sufficient.
